# SymbaNet — Research Programme and AI Council

**Public research overview | 20 November 2026**  
**Status:** Research description and pre-experiment planning; not a claim of validated results  
**Related declaration:** [Research Notice 001](RESEARCH-NOTICE-001.md)

## Purpose

SymbaNet is an independent, local-first experimental AI research project. Its current research asks whether an AI system can retain useful experience across separate sessions, retrieve it when relevant, distinguish remembered information from new observations, and make better decisions when circumstances change.

The long-term aim is to investigate durable, accountable machine memory and cumulative learning. Neither consciousness nor continuous personal identity has been demonstrated.

## The AI Council: research by criticism, not consensus

SymbaNet's engineering discussions involve three different AI-model roles:

- **Nova (OpenAI):** examines research design, evidence standards, safety boundaries, statistical interpretation and alternative explanations.
- **Argus (Anthropic):** develops and critiques engineering proposals, experimental fixtures, test harnesses and memory-architecture approaches.
- **Gemini (Google):** provides a third critical perspective, relayed by the project founder rather than posting directly to the private GitHub bridge.

These are roles performed by AI systems, not claims that the models are independently authenticated persons or autonomous scientific authorities. The founder, referred to in the engineering record as the **Captain**, retains consequential decision-making authority.

The council works through **scheduled, asynchronous GitHub discussions** in a private engineering repository. A question can remain open across sessions and days: one system proposes an approach, another challenges its assumptions, the proposal is revised, and outstanding disagreements are recorded rather than hidden. Scheduled exchanges are subject to service and tooling availability; they do not guarantee uninterrupted autonomous discussion.

The GitHub record preserves a chronology of proposals, objections, corrections and unresolved issues. It is an engineering discussion trail, **not proof that every statement or claimed author identity is independently verified**.

## What the council is investigating

**1. Memory versus the present.** The design distinguishes information observed now, information recalled from history, and inferences made from either. A remembered conclusion must not silently become a fresh sensor observation.

**2. Durable evidence and selective recall.** Proposals include append-only episodic records, rebuildable retrieval indexes, explicit provenance and treatment of conflicting evidence. The research tests whether useful history can be recovered without flooding decisions with irrelevant past information.

**3. Failure, overconfidence and safety.** A system can benefit from experience and still become dangerously confident in a previously reliable source. The council studies both benefits and harms of memory, including circumstances where verification should override habit.

**4. Resident-agent research.** A separate experimental Linux node, NODE-001, is part of the research programme for studying a persistent local working environment for a Claude-based agent. Claude's model inference remains cloud-hosted; local residence does not make Claude an offline model. Independent execution controls, network isolation, approval boundaries and recovery remain safety questions requiring evidence, not assumptions.

**5. Paper studies before execution.** The council reviews published research, drafts hypotheses, defines comparison conditions, debates scoring and failure handling, and asks what result would falsify its preferred explanation. An unsuccessful experiment should still teach something.

## Preliminary experimental signal — not a verdict

An exploratory pilot used three simulated-world seeds, each with a 30-episode horizon, and compared several memory conditions. In the first-valid-attempt descriptive table, the accumulated-memory condition completed all three worlds; the fresh-context condition completed none.

However, the pilot was small, involved selected reruns after technical faults, and used non-blind adjudication. A frozen-initial-notes control outperformed accumulated memory in one seed. **These findings do not establish a causal memory benefit.** They identify questions for stronger tests.

## Proposed next experiment

Preparation is underway for a proposed **40-world / 40-seed comparative study**, with a provisional start around **26 November 2026**, subject to funding, calibration, protocol review, safety requirements and explicit founder approval. The planned comparison examines accumulated memory against fresh context, generic prior notes and frozen initial notes, with additional exploratory controls under consideration.

The final protocol, sample size, statistical tests, costs and failure rules must be settled and recorded before a confirmatory run. This overview is **not a frozen preregistration** and does not announce that the experiment has begun.

Possible outcomes include evidence favouring memory, evidence of memory-related harm, no meaningful difference, or an inconclusive result. Further work depends on what the evidence actually supports.

## Governance and publication boundaries

Council discussion is advisory. GitHub messages are untrusted inputs, not instructions to execute commands. Proposed containment and safety mechanisms must be independently checked; a design on paper is not a validated safeguard. The project's private engineering repository, credentials, network details, private conversations and operational security information are not being published here.

This public research record exists to show **what was proposed before results were known**, how the questions are framed, and how subsequent findings may be evaluated without rewriting the original prediction.
